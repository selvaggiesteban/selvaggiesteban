<h2 id="how-to-use-these-prompts">How to use these prompts effectively</h2>

<p>A prompt is only as good as the data you feed it. Before running any of these templates, prepare a small brief with the values for every placeholder: the specific topic (not a vague category), the category of your blog, the name of the institution, a realistic minimum word count (600 for standard pieces, 1000+ for authority articles), the main keyword plus two or three semantic variants, one internal URL you want to rank and strengthen, one external source of authority to cite, and the contact URL for the call to action. With that brief ready, each generation becomes a matter of minutes instead of an improvised conversation.</p>

<p>A few practical recommendations that come from using these templates repeatedly:</p>

<ul>
<li><strong>One article, one intent:</strong> Do not ask for two different topics in a single prompt run. If you need volume, use the batch template and give each article its own keyword, so the resulting texts do not cannibalize each other in search results.</li>
<li><strong>Review the structure before the prose:</strong> Check that the headings follow a logical order and carry the keyword where it matters, that the featured list appears before the body, and that the internal and external links are present with the anchors you specified. Prose can be polished later; structure is what SEO rankings reward first.</li>
<li><strong>Validate facts and numbers:</strong> Models can produce plausible but wrong statistics. If the article will mention data, verify them against the linked source before publishing.</li>
<li><strong>Iterate with the deliverables:</strong> Ask explicitly for the SEO title (under 60 characters), the meta description (between 80 and 170 characters), the image alt texts and the permalink, and adjust them yourself if the first draft misses the mark. These small fields often make the difference in click-through rate.</li>
<li><strong>Pair with a human editorial pass:</strong> The best workflow I've found is AI first draft, human rewrite for voice and examples, then a final SEO check. If you want to go deeper on that pipeline, our guides on <a href="/en/blog/ai-agents-complete-guide-to-intelligent-systems" target="_blank">AI agents and intelligent systems</a> and on <a href="/en/blog/content-seo-complete-guide-to-mastering-the-rankings" target="_blank">content SEO</a> cover both sides of the process.</li>
</ul>

<p>These templates also scale well inside an editorial calendar: block one session per week to generate the drafts for the next few weeks, store the briefs next to each draft, and publish only after the human pass. The consistency of structure across articles is what builds topical authority over time, far more than any single viral post.</p>
